# Facts and sources — The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| In 1925 Victor Lustig, posing as a government official (Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs), invited scrap-metal dealers to a secret meeting at the Hôtel de Crillon and "sold" the Eiffel Tower | Smithsonian, "The Smoothest Con Man That Ever Lived" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-smoothest-con-man-that-ever-lived-29861908/ ; Eiffel Tower official site, "Who sold the Eiffel Tower?" — https://www.toureiffel.paris/en/news/history-and-culture/who-sold-eiffel-tower |
| The pretext: the tower was costly to maintain and in need of repair | same sources; Mental Floss — https://www.mentalfloss.com/crime/smooth-operator-how-victor-lustig-sold-eiffel-tower |
| The buyer was André Poisson, new to Paris and eager for status; he paid for the tower plus a bribe; he was too embarrassed to go to the police | Smithsonian; Wikipedia, "Victor Lustig" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Lustig |
| Lustig left the country, came back and tried again; this time the police were told and he got away | Wikipedia; Smithsonian (accounts differ on details) |
| Years later he was caught in the United States for counterfeiting | Wikipedia; Kirkus review of the 1961 book — https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/a/neil-hickey-2/the-man-who-sold-the-eiffel-tower |
| "7,000 tons of iron" | the tower's iron structure weighs about 7,300 tonnes (official site) |

Caveats: much of the story comes from a 1961 "as told to" memoir, so the narration says "as the story goes". Not used: exact sums (sources disagree), where he fled (Vienna/Austria in some accounts), the claim that Poisson's wife grew suspicious, and the Alcatraz detail.
