# Facts and sources — Mansa Musa, 1324

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Mansa Musa, ruler of the Mali Empire, made the pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 and passed through Cairo | World History Encyclopedia, "Mansa Musa I" — https://www.worldhistory.org/Mansa_Musa_I/ ; Boston University African Studies, "Kingdom of Mali" — https://www.bu.edu/africa/outreach/k_o_mali |
| "Often called the richest man who ever lived" (a popular title, not a measured fact) | Ancient Origins fact check on the "richest man" claim — https://www.ancient-origins.net/news-history-archaeology/mansa-musa-400-billion-richest-man-fact-check-00103294 |
| He gave away gold in Cairo; so much gold came in that its value fell | al-Umari's account, World History Commons — https://worldhistorycommons.org/al-umaris-account-mansa-musas-visit-cairo |
| "According to one historian, 12 years later it still hadn't recovered" | al-Umari (secondhand, written years later); modern historians (e.g. Warren C. Schultz) question how long the effect lasted — hence "according to one historian" |
| "By some accounts, tens of thousands of people" | numbers vary between accounts; the film says "by some accounts" and shows no figure |
| A famous map of the world, about 50 years later, shows him on a throne holding gold | the Catalan Atlas (1375), widely reproduced; World History Encyclopedia (above) |

Not used: the popular claim that he borrowed money back from Cairo moneylenders (not found in a reliable source).
