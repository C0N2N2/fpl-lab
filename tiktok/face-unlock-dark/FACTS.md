# Facts and sources — Hidden Lines No. 001

| Claim in the film | Source |
|---|---|
| The face-unlock camera system projects and analyses invisible dots to build a depth map of the face, and also captures an infrared image | Apple Support, "About Face ID advanced technology": "projecting and analyzing thousands of invisible dots to create a depth map of your face and also captures an infrared image of your face" — https://support.apple.com/102381 |
| "More than 30,000" dots | Popular Science, on the 2017 launch: the projector "projects a pattern of more than 30,000 infrared dots on the user's face" — https://www.popsci.com/apple-face-ID/ |
| A flood illuminator lights the face with infrared, so it works in the dark | Wikipedia, "Face ID": "a module called the flood illuminator that shines infrared light at the face"; it works in the dark "by invisibly illuminating the whole face with a dedicated infrared flash module" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_ID |
| Infrared is light just past red that your eyes can't see (visible band ≈ 400–700 nm) | standard physics; the ruler shows the band schematically, no exact laser wavelength is claimed |
| The stored map is kept on the phone | Apple Support (same page): face data stays on the device |
| A flat photo gives no depth | follows from the depth map above; the film's "no depth, no unlock" is the general principle, not a test of any specific phone |

Illustration notes: the phone and head are generic and illustrative (no brand); the film shows about 8,200 dots, fewer than the real 30,000+, so they stay visible on a phone screen; the dot grid is regular for clarity (real patterns are pseudo-random).
