# Facts and sources — Hidden Lines No. 002

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Active noise cancelling plays a sound wave with the same amplitude and inverted phase ("anti-noise"); the two combine and cancel (destructive interference) | Wikipedia, "Active noise control" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_noise_control |
| A microphone outside the earcup/earbud picks up ambient noise (feedforward); a microphone inside listens to what reaches the ear and corrects (feedback); many earbuds use both (hybrid) | same article, sections on feedforward / feedback; manufacturers' descriptions of "hybrid ANC" |
| ANC works best on steady low-frequency noise (engines, trains); sudden and high-frequency sounds are handled mostly by passive isolation (the seal of the ear tip) | same article: active control is most effective at low frequencies, passive methods at high frequencies |
| A loud subway car is around 90 dB | typical published measurements of subway noise range about 80–100 dBA; the film says "around 90" |
| "Thousands of times every second" | the microphone signal is sampled tens of thousands of times a second; the film rounds down |
