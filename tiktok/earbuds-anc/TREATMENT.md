# Hidden Lines No. 002 — How noise cancelling works

Style: Night Paper Cutaway (the channel's own style, based on Line-Art Cutaway). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, 76 s. English narration and subtitles. Accent (#FF6A2A) = the anti-noise: the signal path and the opposite wave, and nothing else.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Story bookend**: a commuter on a loud night train presses one button; we dive into her earbud, follow the sound through it, and come back out to her in silence.
2. **Before / after**: the same train ride twice, once with the noise, once without, with the mechanism between.
3. **Journey of one wave**: follow a single wave from the rails to her eardrum.

**Chosen: 1**, with 3 as the middle. The owner asked for more storytelling; a character gives the hook ("the train disappears") and the ending ("Next stop: hers"). The middle follows the wave: outer mic, chip, speaker, the cancelling sum, the inner mic, then the limit (sudden sounds), which is the turn.

## Logline
On the loudest train in the city, her earbuds make the noise vanish, not by blocking it, but by adding its exact opposite.

## Sound
96 bpm. The score stays low and almost absent while the train is loud (a rumble bed plays the train), stops dead at the button (near-silence 1, 10–11.6 s), comes in as soft mallets for the inside, a harp sweep when the waves cancel, near-silence 2 (41.8–43.2 s) before the inner mic, a resolved chord at the bookend. Foley: card taps as slices fold up, paper slides for strips, the train rumble that cuts at the button.
